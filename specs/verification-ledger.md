## 6. Long-form agentic dictation — Agent `task`/`ask` system prompt redesign (2026-07-04)

`[verified]` live Foundation Models, 5 real long-form fixtures (ideation, brownfield-context,
multi-step-out-of-order, debugging-narrative, course-correction), 4 candidate instructions,
greedy decoding. Full method + data table: `research/long-form-agent-dictation-review.md`.

**Finding**: the prior `"Remove filler. 1–3 sentences maximum."` cap on `DefaultProfiles.agent`
silently dropped real, decision-relevant content in 4/5 live cases (scope constraints,
a reusable-existing-component reference, task priority order, a mid-dictation retraction).

**Adopted**: replaced the cap with a density-preserving instruction ("length matches the
input's real content") + an explicit retraction-preservation rule, plus a third few-shot
example anchoring "dense input keeps ALL its constraints" (the two pre-existing examples
were both short and were empirically pulling output back toward brevity even with the new
instruction text — few-shot examples dominate instruction wording for this model).
Re-verified on the full production path (system prompt + real few-shot examples, not the
isolated instruction) before landing. `fix`/`shell`/`code`/`commit` categories untouched —
they are a different, terse medium and already enforce their own strict formats.

**Known open gap** (not fixed this pass): identifiers/component-names mentioned inside a long
hedged aside get dropped by the on-device model regardless of the explicit
"preserve every identifier" instruction (the brownfield-context fixture, `PinnedContextStore`).
Looks like a real 3B-model limitation under hedged phrasing, not a prompt-wording gap.

**Latency tradeoff**: long-form cleanup now costs ~1.5–2s live vs ~0.7–1.5s before (real,
not noise) — acceptable for the depth this buys, but the right lever if it becomes a problem
is investigating `LanguageModelSession` creation cost / reuse, not re-shortening the prompt.

## 7. E7 — `SpeechAnalyzer` partials arrive on a ~1 s grid, which refutes transcript-based endpointing (2026-08-09)

`[verified]` `make probe-latency` (`scripts/probe-partial-latency.swift`), 5 utterances × 2
reporting configurations, run twice with results stable to ~10 ms.

**The measurement nobody had made.** E6 measured volatile→final (174 ms) and
drained-input→final (40–80 ms). Neither is audio→volatile. `EndpointDecider` was built on the
assumption that the transcript is current enough to choose a 200–900 ms silence window from
it, and that assumption had never been tested. E7 tests it by feeding real silence at real
time after the utterance and finalizing only afterwards — E6 called
`finalizeAndFinishThroughEndOfInput()` the instant audio ran out, which forces a flush a live
microphone never provides and so manufactures fast arrivals.

**Finding: partial results are emitted on a ~1.04 s cadence anchored to analyzer start, not to
speech content.** Across utterances of 0.80 s to 3.41 s of audio, every volatile landed in one
of the same five bands: 1137–1232 / 2106–2345 / 3049–3345 / 4267–4283 / 5313–5396 ms. Adding
`ReportingOption.fastResults` moved nothing (L_words 429→431, 639→641, 740→744, 227→227 ms) —
the cadence is structural, not a reporting-option default.

**Consequences, measured from the last frame of speech (= when a live VAD starts counting):**

| signal | latency | the half of `EndpointDecider` it drives | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| last word appears (`L_words`) | +227 … +744 ms | lengthening on a dangling tail — the safety property | **unreliable**: exceeds the 600 ms VAD window in 2 of 5 |
| terminal punctuation on a volatile (`L_punct`) | +1474 … +1693 ms, or never | shortening — the advertised 400 ms win | **refuted**: arrives ~7× later than the 200 ms window it would trigger |

The decider's input is staler than its output. It reads a transcript that is 0–1040 ms behind
in order to choose a window of 200–900 ms, so the text that would justify the decision does not
exist yet when the decision must be made. E6's 174 ms volatile→final gap was real but did not
imply early arrival: under a live mic *both* events sit ~1.5 s past the endpoint.

**Also refuted: the fallback of "key off transcript arrival instead of audio silence."** That
path is bounded below by the same ~1 s cadence, so it is strictly slower than the 600 ms
energy VAD it would replace. Energy-based endpointing is not the weak option here — it is the
fastest signal available on-device.

**Adopted**: `VoiceActivityDetector`'s energy window stays the endpoint. `EndpointDecider`
(204 lines, 20 passing tests, zero production call sites) rests on a premise this experiment
refutes and should not be plugged in; its header claim that punctuation-on-a-volatile is a
usable early signal is `[verified]` as measured by E6 but false as applied.

### 7a. The feed-shape hypothesis, tested and excluded

The one live objection to the above: E7's first run fed the synthesizer's native ~11 ms
buffers, one `AnalyzerInput` yield each. Production is coarser — `AudioCapture` installs its
tap at 4096 frames (`AudioCapture.swift:46`), ~85 ms at a 48 kHz hardware rate, so
`AppleSpeechTranscriber` yields ~8x fewer, ~8x larger inputs. If the analyzer's partial
cadence were driven by input arrival rather than by its own clock, the whole E7 verdict would
be an artifact of the harness rather than a fact about the pipeline.

`scripts/probe-partial-latency.swift` now re-slices the rendered audio to a uniform chunk
size before feeding it, and sweeps that size. Same five utterances, three feeds spanning 23x:

| feed | best `L_words` | worst `L_words` |
|---|---|---|
| native (~11 ms) | +225 ms | +753 ms |
| **85 ms — the production tap shape** | +222 ms | +747 ms |
| 250 ms | +223 ms | +751 ms |

Per-utterance the three agree to within ~6 ms. `fastResults` re-run at the production chunk
size likewise moves nothing (+441 / +645 / +751 / +227 / +421 ms).

**Concluded** `[verified]`: the ~1 s partial cadence is a property of `SpeechAnalyzer`, not of
how audio is handed to it. Feed granularity does not control it across a 23x range, so the
native-buffer measurement in §7 was never a harness artifact and its numbers stand as
production numbers.

**Still `[unverified]`**: a live microphone end to end. But the mechanism by which a live mic
could plausibly have differed — buffer shape and yield frequency — is now excluded, and the
grid is anchored to analyzer start independently of utterance length (0.80 s to 3.41 s). What
remains untested is real prosody, and §7's note applies: a human trailing off quietly is
likely *slower* than synthesized speech, which is the conservative direction. The one cheap
confirmation left is reading partial timestamps off `make logs` during a live dictation.
